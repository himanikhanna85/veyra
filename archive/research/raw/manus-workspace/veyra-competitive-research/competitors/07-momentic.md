# Momentic

**Research date: 6 October 2026.** This profile covers exactly Momentic. Claims are labeled as vendor-marketed, documented capability, independently supported, or analyst inference. Public documentation is evidence of what Momentic says or documents, not a hands-on benchmark.

## Active identity and intended customer

Momentic is an active San Francisco developer-tools company founded in 2023 and a YC Winter 2024 company. Y Combinator currently describes it as “Mo, the AI QA engineer that bug-bashes your app before every release,” and says teams also use Momentic for plain-English end-to-end tests in CI and agent-assisted maintenance across web and mobile. **Independently supported company status:** YC lists the company as Active. [1] The current product is not only the earlier browser-test framework: Momentic’s documentation explicitly distinguishes **Mo**, an exploratory AI QA engineer that starts from a running app and a brief, from repository tests stored as YAML. **Documented capability.** [2]

The intended customer is a product-led engineering or QA team that wants repeatable regression checks without maintaining selector-heavy scripts, and developer teams that want CLI/MCP workflows alongside CI. Momentic explicitly says it is built for engineers and coding agents rather than a purely no-code audience: steps are plain English, tests are YAML in the repository, and the CLI runs in CI. **Vendor-marketed/documented positioning.** [3] The YC profile names Notion, Whatnot, Retool, Webflow, Framer and 1,600+ teams; those are vendor/YC-listed customer claims, not independently audited adoption data. [1]

The brand and product scope have expanded materially from browser-only positioning. Current official docs cover web, iOS and Android, while Mo can explore a running web URL or uploaded mobile build. Web execution remains Chromium-family only; mobile uses simulators/emulators rather than physical devices. [2][4] No acquisition or rebranding was found in the reviewed public sources. The relevant status change is product broadening and the separate Mo product line, not a verified corporate acquisition.

## Distinctive capabilities and mechanism versus the category baseline

The category baseline is code- or selector-oriented browser automation: a person writes explicit steps against CSS/XPath/test IDs, maintains them as the UI changes, and reviews screenshots/logs after a run. Momentic’s distinctive mechanism is to combine human-readable intent with an agent runtime. A test can mix exact preset steps, goal-driven AI actions, and reusable modules. A preset action can remain explicit while using AI to locate a target, evaluate an assertion or extract data; an AI action lets the runtime decide the sequence needed to reach a goal. **Documented capability; distinction is analyst comparison with the baseline.** [5]

Authoring is available through the visual editor, YAML in a repository, a CLI, coding-agent skills and an MCP server. Momentic says plain-English steps are saved as YAML next to application code and add no XPath, CSS paths or test IDs to the app. Its docs also state that Mo does not write YAML automatically during an exploratory session; selected cases can later be turned into repository tests. **Documented capability.** [3][6] This is distinctive from a pure recorder because it treats the repository file as the durable artifact and keeps a human-readable intent layer, but the evidence does not establish that the model’s interpretation is always correct.

Assertions include normal element/text checks and AI or multimodal assertions over the DOM, accessibility tree, screenshot and rendered appearance. The official web page specifically markets checks for color, layout, image content and position, while the results docs show step status, screenshots, traces, video and AI reasoning. **Documented capability.** [3][7] This is broader than a simple click-path recorder, but public material does not prove oracle precision, false-pass rate, or superiority to established visual-testing systems.

Momentic has first-class reusable modules and parameterization. A module is a named sequence of preset steps or AI actions, can be called by tests, and can accept defaults, enum-constrained inputs and invocation-specific values. The docs give login and checkout examples and support cached module invocations and authentication state. **Documented capability.** [8] This overlaps Veyra’s proposed semantic business modules in reuse and parameters, but Momentic’s public module contract is primarily a YAML sequence plus inputs; no public evidence was found of a richer business-domain contract tying module outputs, approved rules, dependencies and risk semantics together.

The most distinctive maintenance stack is layered rather than one undifferentiated “self-heal.” During a run, locator auto-healing re-resolves a target and waits for page/DOM/network/navigation stability without editing the test. Failure recovery can add temporary steps to clear a transient obstruction and retry, again without modifying the test. Permanent healing uses classification and triage to diagnose, re-run candidate repairs and deliver a reviewable patch, pull request or configured alternative. Quarantine contains unresolved flakiness without silently treating it as a healthy pass. **Documented capability.** [9] The docs explicitly say recovery does not hide product regressions, outages, 5xx responses, CAPTCHAs or browser crashes, and that classification can be manually overridden. This is meaningful human-governance evidence, although the default delivery settings and “AI routing” still leave teams to decide how much automation to permit.

Momentic also advertises a step cache, memory, knowledge base and App graph. Successful element resolutions can replay quickly from a cache; memory supplies prior locator/assertion decisions; the knowledge base stores terminology, agent rules and known flows; and the App graph builds a versioned journey model from observed page states. **Documented capability.** [5][10][11][12] The App graph is explicitly alpha and has an approval queue for proposed product areas, features, journeys, variants and segments. Coverage states include Covered, Partial, Quarantined, Missing and Ignored, and AI test selection can use graph links between code changes and product journeys. This is the closest public analogue to Veyra’s longer-term ABM, but it is described as runtime journey coverage and risk context, not a full business-intent/state/dependency/rules model.

Evidence and diagnostics are unusually complete in the public docs: dashboard runs retain videos, traces, screenshots, auto-heal history and quarantine state; each step can expose DOM/network/console traces, cache status, AI reasoning and healing traces. Standard local reporters include JUnit, Allure, Momentic JSON, Playwright JSON and Buildkite JSON. **Documented capability.** [7] Mo reports cases, verdicts, coverage gaps, reproduced bugs, reproduction steps, logs and video replay. [2] These support evidence-backed outcomes, though no public documentation was found for a first-class BLOCKED verdict with Veyra’s exact semantics; statuses documented for repository runs include pass, fail, flaky, quarantined and cancelled.

## Current official pricing and commercial terms

As of the official pricing page last updated 27 September 2026, Momentic is usage-based and has **no per-user seat charges**. [13] The Free plan is **$0 forever**, requires no credit card, and includes 2,000 credits per month (approximately 200 ten-step runs), all core features and AI test authoring, and 30-day results retention. Free usage hard-stops at its quota; credits do not roll over. The page lists five concurrent hosted browsers, one concurrent mobile device, 15-minute mobile sessions, unlimited disposable email inboxes, zero phone numbers, and forever aggregate analytics retention. [13]

The self-serve Pay-as-you-go plan is **$125/month plus additional usage**. This is a true monthly list price as presented, not an annual-commitment equivalent. It includes 10,000 credits/month (approximately 1,000 ten-step runs), no rollover, 25 concurrent hosted browsers, five Android plus five iOS concurrent mobile devices, 15-minute mobile sessions, unlimited email inboxes, five phone numbers, and 30-day full-result retention with forever analytics retention. Additional use is billed at **$0.01875 per credit**; customers can buy 10,000-credit blocks for $125. Enterprise is **custom/test-based pricing**; the public page does not estimate private contract prices. [13]

Credits are the primary billing unit, not seats, test cases or a fixed execution-minute allowance. A normal test step costs one credit; an AI-generated or failure-recovery step costs two. Hosted browser time is one credit/minute; hosted Android emulator time is eight credits/minute; hosted iOS simulator time is 15 credits/minute. Failure classification costs 100 credits/run, triage 500 credits/run and AI test selection 300 credits/run. Interactive editor runs are free. [13] The page says Enterprise usage, overage, concurrency, retention and credit rates are custom. Monthly balances reset at the billing-cycle start and do not roll over. Google SSO is available on all plans; Pay-as-you-go adds domain claiming; Enterprise adds SAML, SCIM, audit logs, higher API limits, priority support, account management, onboarding, SLA and custom terms. [13]

A material pricing contradiction exists in older/secondary material. Bug0’s 31 July 2026 overview says paid pricing was quote-based and not publicly available. [14] That is contradicted by the current official page updated 27 September 2026, which publishes $125/month Pay-as-you-go and exact credit rates. Treat the Bug0 statement as an older or stale observation, not current list pricing. The YC launch post also offered $250 free signup credits, but the current official pricing page instead describes the standing Free plan as 2,000 credits/month and does not present the old $250 offer as a plan term. [1][13]

## Local execution, cloud AI, privacy and portability

Momentic supports local developer workflows: the same CLI/test files can run locally and in CI, and local web authoring/editor/MCP execution uses local Chromium. Hosted browsers can be enabled for `momentic run`; a local or CI worker then drives a Momentic-managed browser, subject to the plan’s concurrency cap. **Documented capability.** [4] The enterprise page says the CLI can run browser/device execution inside the customer’s own machine, CI runner, private VPC or VPN, while hosted runs can reach private applications through a private Connector. It also states that the control plane is not fully on-premise and that data residency depends on which data must remain inside the customer boundary. **Documented capability; important privacy distinction.** [15]

Local browser execution does **not** establish offline or private AI processing. The reviewed public docs explain local versus hosted browser/device execution and authenticated cloud dashboard state, but do not establish that AI models, memory, classification or healing can run fully offline or that prompts/artifacts never leave the customer environment. Treat offline/private AI as **unverified**. Enterprise security claims include SOC 2 Type 2, encryption in transit/at rest, SAML, SCIM, RBAC, audit logs and a contractual 99.99% SLA, but these are vendor-marketed/documented claims unless independently verified through the Trust Center. [15]

Portability is mixed. Tests and modules are YAML files in the customer’s repository, the format is published, and standard result reporters reduce dashboard dependence. However, Momentic explicitly says it ships no exporter; AI actions, AI checks, step cache, classification and auto-maintenance do not map automatically to open-source Playwright. Enterprise customers may ask for a one-time eject to open-source Playwright at contract termination, but this is a contractual enterprise option rather than a general export guarantee. Dashboard history, memory and step caches stay behind when leaving. **Documented capability and lock-in evidence.** [16]

## Moat mechanisms and defensibility

**Accumulated learned artifacts — emerging to moderate, with uncertainty.** The plausible mechanism is compounding organization-specific state: cached successful locators, memory of prior locator/assertion decisions, knowledge-base terminology/rules/flows, App graph journey coverage and failure/healing history. These artifacts can improve consistency and reduce maintenance over time, and leaving forfeits memory/cache/history. [5][10][11][12][16] The grade is not strong because memory expires after 30 days of inactivity, the App graph is alpha, and the public sources do not quantify how much customer-specific data improves outcomes versus a fresh run.

**Switching costs — moderate.** Repository YAML creates a relatively portable source layer, which limits lock-in, but AI actions/checks, cache, memory, cloud history, hosted infrastructure and triage workflows are not fully portable. Teams also accumulate integrations, run history, reviewed healing practices and knowledge entries. [16] The switching cost is therefore operational and informational rather than an irreplaceable file format. An enterprise Playwright eject option further lowers the theoretical ceiling.

**Distribution and workflow position — emerging to moderate.** Momentic is distributed through developer tooling (CLI, MCP, coding-agent skills, GitHub/GitLab and CI), and it can enter both exploratory bug-bashing and pull-request regression. YC and official customer pages provide evidence of customer and ecosystem reach, but those are vendor/YC claims and do not prove durable distribution advantage. [1][3][15]

**Proprietary infrastructure and operating capability — moderate, vendor-claimed.** The company documents hosted browser/device fleets, private Connectors, parallel execution, artifact pipelines, recovery/triage agents and cross-platform orchestration. [4][9][15] These could be difficult to operate reliably at scale, especially for mobile simulators and private networks. The grade remains moderate rather than strong because public material supplies no independent reliability benchmark, infrastructure cost data or proof that the capability is exclusive.

**Data network effects from bugs and runs — unproven to emerging.** Momentic reports large aggregate counts such as 70M+ test runs, 117,010 bugs caught and 8.9M steps auto-healed on its web page. [3] These are vendor-marketed counters; they may indicate operating scale but do not show that one customer’s data trains another customer’s outcomes, and security boundaries may prevent cross-customer learning. Funding, generic LLM access and feature count are not treated as moats.

Overall defensibility is **emerging/moderate, not proven**. The strongest plausible combination is workflow distribution plus accumulated customer-specific run context and a difficult-to-operate execution/diagnostic platform. There is no evidence that Momentic has exclusive rights to the underlying models, intent testing concept or browser automation primitives.

## Limitations, fit and risk for Veyra

Supported limitations include browser coverage: current web docs list Chromium, Google Chrome and Chrome for Testing, while an independent July 2026 overview says Safari and Firefox were not available. [3][14] Momentic’s mobile testing uses simulators/emulators; physical devices are explicitly not supported, which matters for camera, eSIM and biometric flows. [15] Mo exploratory sessions do not directly write YAML, requiring a human/coding-agent conversion step for durable CI coverage. [2][6] Enterprise is not fully on-premise, despite in-network execution options. [15] Momentic ships no automatic converter from Playwright/Cypress/Selenium and no general exporter; leaving loses cloud history, memory and cache. [16] The App graph is alpha. [12]

Momentic also exceeds Veyra’s V0 in several areas: CI and scheduled runs, parallel execution, API/network request mocking, cross-platform mobile support, MCP/coding-agent integration, AI failure classification/triage and cloud-hosted scale. [3][5] This means Veyra should not claim broad feature parity. The fair comparison is a local-first manual/low-code product with explicit assertions, business modules, evidence-backed PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED and human-governed AI, versus an engineering-oriented platform that optimizes intent-driven automation and maintenance.

The principal Veyra opportunity is not “AI browser testing” in general; Momentic already occupies that phrase. A plausible wedge is a trustworthy manual-to-repeatable loop: **Teach -> Test -> Results**, where every assertion has explicit oracle semantics, evidence is attached to the verdict, BLOCKED is a first-class non-pass state, and AI suggestions are proposed for human approval rather than silently changing test behavior. Momentic has related pieces—plain-English assertions, reviewable healing, knowledge approvals and YAML modules—but no reviewed public evidence of Veyra’s exact governance model or business-semantic contract.

Veyra’s longer-term ABM could be distinctive if it turns approved business intent, inputs/outputs, state transitions, dependencies, risk and failure knowledge into a durable, inspectable graph rather than primarily a runtime journey graph. Momentic’s App graph is the nearest documented analogue, with journey variants, coverage states, proposals and risk context, so uniqueness should be described as **distinctive**, not exclusive. [12] Veyra should assume Momentic can converge toward semantic models and should focus on explainability, local-first privacy boundaries, human approval workflows and low-code tester usability.

Veyra risks include Momentic’s established developer distribution, broad execution infrastructure, public customer references, free tier and clear credit pricing; Momentic could also compress Veyra’s wedge by adding stronger approval and business-rule layers. Conversely, Veyra’s local-first stance may appeal where cloud control-plane dependence, cloud AI ambiguity, network perimeter concerns or enterprise data residency are unacceptable. That fit is an inference, not evidence of unmet demand. Public research did not reveal independently measured Momentic false-pass/false-fail rates, real-world BLOCKED semantics, full offline AI, or customer-by-customer data isolation details.

## Evidence gaps and source notes

The reviewed public sources do not provide a hands-on benchmark, independent pricing invoice, independent reliability study, public model/provider disclosure, detailed data-retention policy for prompts and screenshots, or proof of a fully offline/private AI mode. Public claims about bugs caught, test-run counts, healing percentages, customer outcomes and parallel capacity remain vendor-marketed unless separately labeled above. The independent Bug0 overview is useful for corroboration and for the older pricing/browser snapshot, but it contains claims that conflict with the later official pricing page and should not override the current primary source. No public evidence of acquisition or corporate rebrand was found.
