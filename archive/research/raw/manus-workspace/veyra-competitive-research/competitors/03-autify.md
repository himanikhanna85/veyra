# Autify

**Research date: 6 October 2026.** This profile covers exactly one competitor, Autify, and separates the company’s currently named products from the older/continuing no-code product family. Claims are labelled as vendor-marketed, documented capability, independently supported, or analyst inference. A public page is not a hands-on benchmark; no Autify account, trial, purchase, or product run was used.

## Active product identity and intended customer

Autify is now a product family rather than one unambiguous browser-testing SKU. The corporate site currently presents **Autify Aximo** as an autonomous AI testing agent, **Autify Genesis** as an AI-driven test-design layer, and **Autify Nexus** as a Playwright-based AI test-automation product. It also continues to link **Autify NoCode** and its separate NoCode Web/NoCode Mobile help and pricing properties. The corporate navigation still lists Autify Mobile and Autify Nexus, while the current home page labels Aximo and Genesis as the headline offerings. This is documented vendor positioning, not evidence that every historical SKU has been retired. [1]

For Veyra’s no-code/AI browser-testing comparison, the closest active match is **Autify NoCode Web** (formerly Autify for Web, with the umbrella NoCode naming also covering Mobile), with **Nexus** relevant as the newer low-code/code-capable browser automation direction. Aximo is materially different: it is an agent that executes natural-language tasks across web, mobile and desktop rather than a conventional recorded scenario editor. Genesis is test design/requirements-to-test-case generation rather than the browser execution engine. Autify’s 2021 launch announcement described Autify for Web as a no-code web application for creating and running tests without programming and introduced Autify for Mobile as a separate native-app product; current pages therefore show a real naming/product evolution rather than a single clean rebrand. [2]

The intended customer is QA, engineering, product and manual-testing teams that need end-to-end/regression/smoke/visual coverage without requiring every author to write automation code. Aximo targets teams willing to give an AI agent natural-language goals and let it navigate; Nexus targets teams wanting no/low-code authoring but Playwright control, local execution, code export and CI/CD; NoCode Web targets business-facing testers and QA teams using a recorder, scenario editor and managed cloud execution. These are vendor descriptions; the corporate site cites customer case studies and an authoring-time reduction, but those are vendor case-study claims rather than independent benchmark results. [1]

## Distinctive capabilities and mechanism versus the category baseline

**NoCode Web’s baseline-plus mechanism is recorded browser scenarios with AI-assisted maintenance.** The recorder captures clicks, inputs and validations into editable steps; the product documentation exposes explicit assertion steps against elements and pages, plus visual-regression capability. The help center describes AI as maintaining scenarios, while the original product announcement claimed auto-repair that detects source/UI changes and corrects scenarios. The public documentation does not fully specify the ranking model, confidence threshold, candidate locators, or whether a repair is automatically committed or presented for approval. Therefore “AI maintenance/auto-repair” is vendor-marketed and partially documented, while its internals and governance controls are unverified. [3][4]

The authoring loop is more mature than a bare recorder. Autify documents **Local Replay/Record here**, which replays prior steps locally to let a tester insert or alter later steps without manually traversing the whole flow. Importantly, the same documentation warns that Local Replay is not test execution: assertions and visual checks are ignored during replay, timing/site behavior can differ, and tracked elements are not automatically maintained during replay. This is a useful authoring mechanism, but it is not proof of equivalent local execution or offline privacy. [5]

Autify has explicit test oracles, but they are conventional UI assertions rather than a documented semantic business contract. The assertion documentation distinguishes assertions on page elements from assertions against the page, and the help index includes visibility, text/value and page-title-style checks. The product also supports visual regression. That maps to Veyra’s explicit assertions and evidence-backed result ambitions, but the public material does not show a business-intent/object-state/dependency graph comparable to the proposed ABM. [4]

**Reusable modules and parameterization are documented.** NoCode Web Step Groups are reusable series of recorded steps that can be inserted into multiple scenarios; edits propagate to scenarios using the group, and a late-2025 article documents overriding input values per scenario. CSV-backed Data lets one scenario run once per data row, with columns exposed as variables in input steps. These are meaningful reusable workflow/data primitives, although they are not documented as typed semantic modules with pre/postconditions, approved business rules, output contracts or dependency declarations. [6][7]

**Diagnostics/evidence are a concrete strength.** Autify documents per-step screenshots and PDF export of test-result detail, with PDF export described as a paid option where contracted. The legacy help center also documents video-result functionality, screenshot bulk download, traces/main logs in the current Nexus pricing/feature description, and plan-dependent result-log retention; the NoCode FAQ says retention can be one month, three months or longer by contract. Evidence is therefore available, but retention and export entitlements vary by product/contract. There is no public proof that Autify’s evidence is automatically tied to a business-level assertion explanation or produces Veyra-style PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED reason codes. [8][9][10]

**Aximo is the most distinctive current mechanism.** Its page says it does not rely on scripts or selectors; the user describes a scenario in plain English, and Aximo uses natural-language interaction/visual recognition to navigate, enter data, execute and verify across web, mobile and desktop. The corporate home page UI example shows sessions, cases, results, memories, model selection and credit consumption, and the pricing FAQ defines credits as per-AI-test-execution step, model/platform dependent. This is vendor-marketed capability, not independently verified robustness. It is distinctive relative to recorder-first tools because intent is the primary input and the agent chooses interaction paths, but it also raises oracle, reproducibility, auditability and cost-control questions for a human-governed QA product. [1][11]

**Nexus is the portability/control counterweight.** The current product page documents a natural-language recorder, requirements/PRD/user-story test-case generation, “Fix with AI” that flags a failed locator and asks whether AI should find an alternative, low-code/full-code step switching, native Playwright architecture, local test execution, cloud execution, on-prem support in the full version, and editable Playwright export/import. This is materially more explicit about human approval than Aximo’s autonomous positioning and is a strong portability story. Still, “no vendor lock-in” is vendor language: exported code improves portability, but scenarios, run history, AI context, screenshots, step-group semantics and cloud configuration may remain migration costs. [12]

## Current official pricing, trial and commercial terms

Pricing is currently split by product and the public pages are in transition. The main **Autify pricing page** publishes Aximo and Nexus prices in **USD** and separately retains a legacy-looking add-on grid for users, cloud parallels, shared remote workspaces, GenAI and IP whitelisting. The page does not state a universal per-minute or per-execution price; Aximo uses **credits per AI execution step**, while concurrency is the number of simultaneous executions. [13]

For **Aximo**, the public page lists a Free Individual User tier at **$0**, with **2,000 one-time credits that do not expire**, one web and one mobile concurrency, and cloud hosting. Core/Starter Teams is **$99/month billed annually** or **$120/month billed monthly**, including **72,000 annual credits** or **6,000 monthly credits**, five web and two mobile concurrent executions, cloud hosting, and web/mobile testing; the page lists desktop testing as Enterprise-only. Team is **$450/month billed annually** or **$550/month billed monthly**, including **360,000 annual credits** or **30,000 monthly credits**, 20 web and five mobile concurrency. Enterprise is quote-based with custom credits/concurrency and on-prem or dedicated infrastructure. Add-on credits exist for paid plans but the page says to contact sales for pricing; no overage amount is published. Credit consumption is per AI execution step: the current FAQ says Sonnet 5.5 is 1x web/1.5x mobile, while Haiku 4.5 and Kimi K2.6 are 0.5x web/1x mobile. These are list prices and quotas shown publicly on the page as of the research date; they may change, and the model names/versions should not be generalized beyond the page. [13]

For **Nexus**, the same official page lists a **14-day trial** and a Free Tier for one individual user. The page shows **$3,600/year starting price** or **$400/month starting price** for Professional, with the annual figure explicitly an annual commitment and the monthly figure a true monthly presentation; it does not publish a per-test, per-minute or execution quota because the listed trial/free capabilities include unlimited tests and unlimited scenarios locally. The free tier includes local environment, local test execution, unlimited tests/scenarios, test plans, no-code/code assertions, URL replacements, Chrome/Edge, browser language/time zone, organization, code steps, trace/main debugging logs and visual regression. Professional adds one shared workspace, sharing/download, Playwright import/export and support; Enterprise is contact-sales with customizable users/workspaces/cloud parallels, on-prem testing and enterprise support. The page separately lists optional published add-on rates for Professional: **$2,400/user/year or $250/user/month**, **$1,200/cloud parallel/year or $150/month**, **$1,200/shared remote workspace/year or $100/month**, and **$600/IP whitelisting/year or $50/month**; GenAI is contact sales, described as a flat cost with a monthly rate limit. The page’s feature-grid copy contains a minor inconsistency (“parallel” in one shared-workspace line), so those add-on units should be confirmed with Autify before procurement. [13]

For **NoCode Web**, the separate official pricing page is **quote-based** for Standard, Advanced and Enterprise; it presents feature entitlements but no public currency/list price. Standard includes Chrome, visual regression, execution API/CI-CD integration, Slack/external integrations, online chat support and five parallel executions; Advanced adds Edge, fixed IP, internal/local-environment testing and CSM onboarding, with ten parallels; Enterprise is custom (10+ parallels per workspace), adds roles, SSO, premier support and other enterprise options. Cross-browser breadth, fixed IP, extra parallels/workspaces, longer result retention and professional services are shown as add-ons or contract-dependent. This is current official availability information, not a price estimate. [14]

The same NoCode pricing page separately presents **Autify for Mobile** pricing: Starter **$199/month**, or **$149/month billed annually** (save up to 18%), one parallel, 250 tests/month, one user and 30-day retention; Pro **$599/month**, or **$499/month billed annually**, one parallel, unlimited tests and users, 30-day retention; Enterprise is custom. It lists cloud testing, recorder, scheduled test plans, REST API and email testing, with several browser/device/host/connect options as add-ons. These mobile numbers should not be incorrectly used as NoCode Web pricing. [14]

Older public material is contradictory because Autify has reworked names and packaging. The NoCode page retains an older plan structure and quote-based web pricing, while the 2026 corporate page publishes Aximo/Nexus seat, credit and concurrency pricing. A 2021 official announcement also described the earlier Autify for Web/Autify for Mobile family. The material conclusion is not that one page is false: the products and commercial model changed. Use the current corporate Aximo/Nexus page for those products and treat NoCode Web’s public price as quote-only unless a current contract confirms otherwise. No private-contract price, review example or search snippet was treated as a list price. [2][13][14]

## Evidence-backed dimensions relevant to Veyra

**Recorder and intent authoring:** NoCode Web has a documented visual recorder and editable scenario steps; Local Replay speeds insertion and editing. Nexus adds natural-language recording and requirements-to-test generation. Aximo makes plain-language intent the agent input, with visual recognition execution. Claims about semantic intent quality, repeatability and edge-case coverage remain vendor-marketed/unverified. [5][12][11]

**Assertions/oracles:** Explicit element/page assertions and visual regression are documented. Data-driven scenario execution is documented. There is no public documentation of a typed semantic contract for a business module, a formal state machine, a dependency graph, or an assertion taxonomy that distinguishes business failure from environment blockage. Aximo says it verifies expected outcome, but public material does not disclose how expected outcomes are represented or audited. [4][7][11]

**Reusable modules and semantic contracts:** NoCode Step Groups are reusable recorded step sequences, and per-scenario input overrides are documented. Nexus advertises reusable test steps/shared step groups. These support mechanical reuse and switching-cost accumulation, but not the proposed Veyra concept of parameterized business modules with explicit inputs/outputs, preconditions, approved rules and risk metadata. That gap is an analyst comparison, not a claim that Autify cannot implement it privately. [6][12]

**Datasets:** NoCode’s CSV Data feature runs a scenario per row, exposes columns as variables and documents parallel/sequential behavior depending on execution mode. This is a clear parameterization primitive; the public page does not establish richer fixture lifecycle, generated-data governance, cross-scenario joins, or data masking semantics. [7]

**Self-healing and approval controls:** NoCode markets AI auto-repair/maintenance; legacy material says AI can correct scenarios when source/UI changes. Nexus documents a more controlled “Fix with AI” flow that flags a failure and asks whether AI should find an alternative locator. Public docs do not expose repair diffs, confidence scores, audit logs, policy gates or a guarantee that autonomous Aximo changes are never committed without review. Treat approval governance as partly supported for Nexus and unverified for NoCode/Aximo. [2][12]

**Diagnostics, evidence, defect memory and coverage:** Screenshots, PDF results, video-result functionality, trace/main logs and retention are documented across the family, with paid/contract-dependent exports and retention. Aximo’s home-page UI visibly includes “Memories,” but no public documentation establishes that this is historical defect memory, learned business behavior, coverage memory or a durable per-application model. Genesis’s requirements-to-test-case generation may increase test-design coverage, but no independent coverage-quality result is available. Veyra should not infer an ABM equivalent from the product word “Memories.” [1][8][9][10]

**Local execution, privacy and AI processing:** NoCode Local Replay is local authoring/replay, not local test execution. Autify Connect creates a secure tunnel from a local client to Autify so cloud execution can reach a private app, but its docs explicitly say it is not a local test-execution system and does not store scenarios/results locally. Nexus explicitly advertises local execution and on-prem support in the full version; its core-feature trial lacks cloud access and AI features by default. None of these statements proves that AI inference is offline, that telemetry is absent, or that all artifacts remain local. Local browser/test execution must not be conflated with offline/private AI processing. [5][15][12]

**Portability and lock-in:** Nexus’s editable Playwright import/export is documented and materially reduces engine lock-in for executable tests. NoCode has scenario/result sharing and downloads, but the public pages do not establish a complete open schema export of scenarios, step groups, data bindings, evidence, history or repair decisions. Aximo’s pricing page says generated scripts via MCP can be produced in a user’s framework and owned by the user; this is promising portability, but the agent’s proprietary context/memory and cloud execution remain potential switching costs. [13][12]

## Plausible moat mechanisms and strength

**Accumulated learned artifacts — emerging, uncertain.** Autify has operated NoCode since 2019, and its products accumulate customer scenarios, locator/maintenance outcomes, step groups, datasets and possibly Aximo “Memories.” If those artifacts are used in a privacy-preserving way to improve repairs or agent execution, they could create a learning loop. Public sources do not establish data scale, cross-customer training, retention, model advantage or whether customer artifacts are portable, so this is an analyst hypothesis rather than a demonstrated moat.

**Switching costs — moderate for NoCode/Nexus, emerging for Aximo.** A large library of recorded scenarios, Step Groups, CSV data, test plans, integrations, result history and team workflows is costly to reconstruct. Nexus code export and Playwright foundation deliberately reduce engine lock-in, which weakens proprietary captivity but can strengthen adoption. Aximo’s cloud credits, agent context, memories and execution history could create deeper dependence, but that mechanism is not documented enough to grade strong.

**Distribution and operating capability — moderate.** Autify has a multi-year global product, enterprise support/CSM options, private-network tunnelling, device/browser execution and a documented customer-success motion. The 2021 announcement reported named customers and $10M Series A funding; these support organizational reach, not a product moat by themselves. The ability to operate secure cross-browser/mobile/desktop execution and provide support can be difficult to replicate operationally, but public independent evidence is limited. [2]

**Proprietary infrastructure — emerging to moderate.** Managed cloud browser/device environments, Connect tunnelling, parallel orchestration, evidence capture, locator maintenance and Aximo’s agent execution stack represent real infrastructure and operational know-how. They are not simply generic LLM access. However, public pages do not disclose architecture, reliability, unit economics, exclusivity, or comparative performance; the grade remains cautious.

**Human-in-the-loop QA workflow — unproven as a moat.** Nexus’s AI suggestion/approval orientation and Autify’s customer-success positioning could make governance and adoption sticky. But no public evidence shows that Autify has a proprietary approved-rules corpus, business-behavior graph, risk model or regulated audit workflow. Veyra’s proposed ABM is therefore a plausible differentiation opportunity rather than something Autify is proven unable to build.

Overall moat assessment: **moderate operating/distribution and switching-cost base; emerging AI/data/infrastructure moat; no publicly proven exclusive moat.** Funding, generic LLM access and feature count are not counted as moats.

## Supported limitations, inference and fit/risk for Veyra

Supported limitations include: NoCode Web public price is quote-based; execution and evidence retention/features vary by plan; Local Replay differs from test execution and ignores assertions/visual checks; Autify Connect is a tunnel to cloud execution, not local execution or local artifact storage; NoCode data is CSV-row-oriented; and some high-value exports/evidence are paid or contract-dependent. The NoCode recorder documentation also lists operations/elements that cannot be recorded as expected, but the public page was not fully fetched here, so no specific unsupported element list is asserted. [5][15][8][14]

It is an analyst inference—not a documented product absence—that the public Autify materials do not show a Veyra-style ABM linking business intent, typed inputs/outputs, application states, dependencies, approved rules, historical defects and risk. Likewise, no public evidence establishes a human approval gate for every AI locator/flow repair, evidence-backed BLOCKED semantics, or durable defect-memory/coverage graph. Missing documentation is not proof of missing capability.

Fit is strongest where Veyra wants a familiar **Teach -> Test -> Results** loop for manual/low-code browser testers: Autify demonstrates that recorder-based scenarios, explicit assertions, reusable groups, datasets, visual checks and rich execution evidence are market-legible. Veyra should treat these as category baseline, not unique moat. Autify’s Nexus provides a direct warning that local execution plus Playwright export can be a compelling portability/privacy posture; Veyra’s local-first design should explicitly explain whether AI is local, remote, or optional and what data leaves the device.

The clearest Veyra opportunity is not “no-code + AI” alone. It is a governed, business-semantic layer: reusable modules whose contracts name business intent and accepted outputs; explicit PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED with evidence and reasons; human-approved AI suggestions with diffs/confidence/audit history; and a durable ABM connecting behavior, defects and risk. Veyra should avoid claiming exclusivity or a proven moat until it demonstrates that this graph improves maintenance, diagnosis and coverage in real customer workflows. Autify’s breadth also creates competitive risk: Aximo can reposition the category around autonomous natural-language execution, while Nexus can meet code-oriented teams with Playwright portability and local/on-prem options. Veyra’s V0 exclusions (API, multi-browser, CI, scheduling, parallel, autonomous exploration and full self-healing) mean it should win a narrower, evidence/governance-first wedge rather than feature-match Autify.

## Material evidence gaps and research limitations

Autify’s current public web presence is internally transitional: NoCode Web/Mobile help and pricing remain live while the corporate site foregrounds Aximo, Genesis and Nexus. The old/new pages do not provide a single migration matrix, deprecation timeline or canonical mapping of “Autify for Web,” “Autify NoCode Web,” “Autify Nexus,” “Autify Genesis” and “Aximo.” The Aximo product is comparatively new in public positioning; there is no independent hands-on benchmark here for agent success, repeatability, false passes, cost per workflow or maintenance reduction. The corporate performance numbers are vendor claims. The TechCrunch result was blocked to text extraction, so no claim relies on it. No signup/trial was performed, and no private quote was requested. Pricing should be rechecked at procurement time because the official page is dynamic and several legacy/modern grids coexist.
